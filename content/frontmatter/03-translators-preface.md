
# Translator's Preface {.unnumbered}

In the Name of Allāh, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful. All praise
belongs to Allāh, Who revealed the Criterion to His servant so that he might be
a warner to all the worlds. Blessings and peace be upon the one whom Allāh sent
as a mercy to the worlds, a guide, a bearer of glad tidings, a warner, a caller
to Allāh by His permission, and an illuminating lamp, our master Muḥammad
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}.

The Quran is the Book of Allāh, the fountainhead of guidance for humankind, and
the means of true success in this world and the Hereafter. Yet despite its
central place in the life of every Muslim, many who sincerely wish to establish
a meaningful relationship with the Quran find themselves uncertain about where
to begin. They listen to a lecture here, watch a YouTube video there, enrol in a
course, read a translation, consult a commentary, and move from one resource to
another, often without knowing how these pieces fit together or what path should
lead them towards a sound understanding of the Book of Allāh.

The difficulty is not always a lack of sincerity. More often, it is the absence
of a method.

The Quran is not an ordinary text whose meanings can be fully grasped through
vocabulary and translation alone. It has its own principles, terminology,
context, sciences, and modes of expression. Its verses explain one another, its
meanings are illuminated by the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allāh
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}, and the understanding of the Companions \hon{\P_THM}{\BODY}
and the generations that followed them forms an essential part of the
interpretive tradition of this Ummah. To approach the Quran without these
foundations can easily lead to confusion, superficiality, or conclusions that
the Quran itself does not support.

This is where the present work acquires its particular importance.

*How To Study The Quran?: 12 Principles for Understanding the Quran* seeks to
provide the sincere seeker with a methodology and blueprint for approaching the
Quran. It does not merely encourage the reader to read the Quran, nor does it
seek to replace the vast heritage of Tafsīr produced by the scholars of Islam.
Rather, it explains how the student should approach the Book of Allāh, what
qualities and foundations are required, which principles must be observed, and
which intellectual and spiritual tools are necessary for deriving true benefit
from it.

The work takes the reader through the essential foundations of Quranic
understanding: recognising the greatness of the Quran, maintaining attachment to
the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allāh \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}, understanding the
purposes of the Quran, determining the meanings of its words and verses, dealing
responsibly with transmitted reports, developing a sound command of the Arabic
language, understanding the history of the nations and religions addressed by
the Quran, and recognising and responding to objections raised against it.

These are not merely academic disciplines. They are safeguards for the seeker.
They protect the reader from reducing the Quran to personal opinion, isolated
quotations, passing impressions, or whatever interpretation happens to be
popular at a particular time.

The author, Shaykh Muḥammad Owais al-Nagrāmī al-Nadwī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, was
particularly qualified to undertake this task. A distinguished scholar, exegete,
teacher, and Shaykh al-Tafsīr of Dār al-ʿUlūm Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ, Lucknow, he
devoted his life to the teaching and dissemination of the Quran and Sunnah. He
had himself benefited greatly from ʿAllāmah Sayyid Sulaymān al-Nadwī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, and his scholarly life was deeply connected with the study,
teaching, and explanation of the Quran.

The preservation and translation of this work also forms part of a wider effort
to preserve, translate, and promote the scholarly legacy of the scholars of
Nagrām. Much of this inheritance was never preserved adequately. Numerous works
remained in manuscript form, while others were published only once or became
increasingly difficult to obtain. Yet the scholarship of these men continued to
benefit students and readers long after their passing.

The idea for this translation initiative began with a request from my late
grandmother, Sultana Fāṭima \hon{\M_HER}{\BODY}, the daughter of Shaykh Owais
al-Nagrāmī, who wished to see her father's works preserved and made accessible
to a wider readership. Her request became the beginning of an undertaking that
has since developed into a continuing effort to recover, translate, and
disseminate the scholarly heritage of Nagrām.

The first fruit of this effort was the first English translation of any of
Shaykh Owais al-Nagrāmī's works, _The Pathway to the Prophet: A Beginner's Guide
to the Science of Ḥadīth_. This was followed by the translation of his renowned
_Taʿlīm al-Qurʾān_, published as _The Quranic Blueprint: Guidance at Every
Step_, and by the translation of a century-old work by Shaykh ʿAbd al-Raḥmān
al-Nagrāmī al-Nadwī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, titled _Women in Islam_.

The present work continues that journey, and in many respects returns us to its
very source: the Quran itself.

There is a profound lesson in preserving the works of scholars. People may be
remembered for generations through their descendants, their institutions, or the
positions they held, but the most enduring legacy of a scholar is often what he
leaves behind in his books. This reminds me of the words of Shaykh Muḥammad ʿAlī
Ādam al-Ityūbī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, who said,

To translate a work, therefore, is not simply to transfer words from one
language into another. It is to preserve a scholarly voice, make its learning
accessible to a new generation, and allow a work that might otherwise remain
confined to a particular language and place to continue serving those for whom
it was written.

For the reader who has long wished to understand the Quran but has never known
where to begin, for the student who has moved between translations, lectures,
courses, and commentaries without a clear framework, and for anyone who wishes
to move from a superficial acquaintance with the Quran towards a deeper and more
disciplined relationship with it, this book offers a starting point.

It does not promise a shortcut to understanding the Quran. Rather, it teaches
the seeker how to begin the journey correctly.

The ultimate purpose of studying the Quran is not merely to know what it says,
but to allow it to reform what we are. Its verses should correct our beliefs,
purify our hearts, shape our character, regulate our conduct, and direct our
lives towards the pleasure of Allāh. Knowledge of the Quran must ultimately lead
to a relationship with the Quran, and that relationship must ultimately lead to
action.

The first step, therefore, is to begin. But to begin with sincerity, sound
principles, and proper guidance. This book seeks to show the way.
