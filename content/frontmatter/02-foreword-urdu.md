Even if the recitation of the Noble Quran is undertaken solely for the purpose
of earning reward and blessing, while being limited to its outward recitation,
it still has its own etiquettes and principles. If, however, understanding the
Quran, even to some degree, is also intended, then these etiquettes and
principles naturally become all the more important, and their list
correspondingly more extensive.

The foremost prerequisite is that the heart of the reader be firmly connected to
the Noble Quran. The degree of humility, reverential awe, and
broken-heartedness, or alternatively, eagerness and spiritual joy, present
within the heart during recitation, together with the extent to which one
remains conscious that this is the Speech of Allāh, will determine the measure
in which one benefits from this treasure-house of guidance. In the same
proportion, the meanings, objectives, and profound insights of this Wise Book
will continue to unfold before the reader.

Taqwá and purity, to whatever extent one is able to attain them, constitute the
primary and indispensable condition for understanding the Quran. A person whose
life is wholly at variance with the Sharīʿah, and who knowingly immerses himself
in sin and immorality, can never truly comprehend the Noble Quran, even were he
to spend his entire lifetime attempting to do so.

There are also other conditions of a lesser order, including familiarity with
the Arabic language, a sound foundation in religious learning and upbringing,
knowledge of the historical context, namely, an understanding of the
circumstances, customs, and modes of life of both the believers and the
disbelievers at the time of the revelation of the Quran, as well as an
acquaintance with the beliefs and practices of other religious communities.

There was a genuine need for a guidebook, authored by a specialist in the field,
for those who seek to benefit from this Divine Book. It gives me great pleasure
to state that this need has now been fulfilled by a graduate of Nadwat
al-ʿUlamāʾ who is also a distinguished man of letters. Not only is he the
teacher of Tafsīr at Dār al-ʿUlūm Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ, possessing many years of
experience in teaching the Quran, but by virtue of his scholarly taste and
intellectual insight, he is, in the truest sense, both a student and a scholar
of the Quranic sciences. His reading in this field is extensive, and, by the
grace of Allāh, his insight into its subjects is remarkably profound.

He is also the compiler of _Tafsīr Ibn al-Qayyim_, a work that he assembled
through painstaking research, carefully extracting exegetical material from
numerous books with exceptional diligence before arranging and publishing it. In
many other respects as well, he has devoted himself to the service of the Quran.

Although this book is concise, it is fully adequate for its intended subject. It
represents a substantial and noteworthy addition to the list of his valuable
contributions to the service of the Quran.
