
# 10. The History of Nations and Religions

It is essential for the student of the Noble Quran to possess a thorough
knowledge of the nations mentioned in the Quran, the religions and communities
to whom it is addressed, and the history of the Prophetic era. The deeper this
study, the more accurately the meanings of the Quranic verses will become
manifest.

The reality is that the Noble Quran addressed, in particular, the four misguided
groups that existed at the time of its revelation: the Mushrikūn (polytheists),
the Jews, the Christians, and the Munāfiqūn (hypocrites). It subjected their
beliefs to critical examination and exposed the corruption of their deeds and
moral conduct.

Consequently, anyone who is unfamiliar with the doctrines of these groups, or
who lacks an understanding of their moral and political life, cannot properly
appreciate the significance of the Quran's style of expression, its manner of
address, or the force of its arguments in the verses concerning them.

Similarly, the Noble Quran addresses the Muslims. It provides guidance regarding
beliefs (_ʿaqāʾid_), acts of worship (_ʿibādāt_), character (_akhlāq_), and
social dealings (_muʿāmalāt_). It also recounts the military expeditions
(_ghazawāt_) and other major events of that period.

If a person is unfamiliar with the historical circumstances surrounding the
revelation of the Quran, he will be unable to place these verses in their proper
context. Rather, there is every possibility that he may fall into error.

It is for this reason that Imām al-Shāṭibī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} states in
_al-Muwāfaqāt_, "Whoever wishes to understand the Noble Quran must possess
knowledge of the circumstances of its revelation."

Expounding this principle, Imām al-Shāṭibī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} makes a
particularly subtle observation. He explains that the sciences of Maʿānī and
Bayān are founded upon possessing accurate knowledge of the discourse itself,
the speaker, and the one being addressed. This is what the scholars refer to as
_muqtaḍā al-ḥāl_ (the contextual requirements of the situation).

Knowledge of the occasions of revelation (_asbāb al-nuzūl_) is, in reality,
knowledge of this _muqtaḍā al-ḥāl_.

Imam al-Shāṭibī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} further observes that ignorance of these
contextual circumstances often gives rise to serious interpretive difficulties.

In summary, familiarity with the conditions of the Muslims during the period of
revelation, together with an understanding of the beliefs, customs, traditions,
and social practices of the communities addressed by the Quran, is
indispensable.

Accordingly, Imām al-Shāṭibī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} also states, "A student's
unfamiliarity with the customs and conventions of the Arabs sometimes leads him
into such serious difficulties that the only way to resolve them is to remove
that ignorance itself."
