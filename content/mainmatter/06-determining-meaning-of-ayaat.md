
# 6. Determining the Meaning of the Quranic Verses

In determining the meaning of the verses of the Noble Quran, the following
principle must always remain in view: the most reliable method is to explain the
Quran through the Quran itself, then through the Sunnah, and thereafter through
the statements of the Ṣaḥābah \hon{\P_THM}{\BODY} and the Tābiʿūn
\hon{\M_THM}{\BODY}.

<!-- TODO ref Ibn Taymiyah Muqaddimah fi usul al-tafsir -->

Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} writes,

> The best method of Tafsīr is that the Quran should be explained by the Quran
> itself. What is stated briefly in one place will be explained in detail
> elsewhere. What is concise in one passage will be elaborated in another. If
> this does not suffice, then one should turn to the Sunnah, for it explains and
> interprets the Quran. Indeed, Imam Abū ʿAbdillāh Muḥammad ibn Idrīs al-Shāfiʿī
> \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} went so far as to say that whatever ruling the Messenger
> of Allāh \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} gave was itself derived from the Quran.
> (Muqaddimah fī Uṣūl al-Tafsīr)

The verses of the Noble Quran resemble and reinforce one another. They explain,
confirm, and complement each other. Allāh Most High says,

\AYAH_AR_START
ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ أَحۡسَنَ ٱلۡحَدِيثِ كِتَٰبٗا مُّتَشَٰبِهٗا مَّثَانِيَ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Allāh has sent down the finest discourse: a Book, consistent in its verses, and
repeating its teachings. (Sūrah al-Zumar, Q39:23)
\AYAH_END

Imam Shāh Walī Allāh al-Dihlawī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} expresses this principle as
follows, "The verses of the Quran resemble one another; one verse confirms
another, and the primary interpreter of the Mighty Quran is the Messenger of
Allāh \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}." (Izālat al-Khafāʾ)

Accordingly, every verse should be understood in the light of the interpretation
supported by other Quranic verses and the Sunnah. If this principle is
neglected, one is liable to stumble at every step.

For example, concerning the Jews, Allāh says in Sūrah al-Baqarah,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَضُرِبَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلذِّلَّةُ وَٱلۡمَسۡكَنَةُ وَبَآءُو بِغَضَبٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِۚ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
They were stamped with humiliation and misery, and incurred the wrath of Allāh.
(Sūrah al-Baqarah, Q2:61)
\AYAH_END

Since the establishment of the State of Israel, questions have repeatedly been
raised: If the Quran foretold humiliation and disgrace for the Jews, how have
they come to possess political authority? The answer is found elsewhere in the
Quran. In Sūrah Āl ʿImrān, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
ضُرِبَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلذِّلَّةُ أَيۡنَ مَا ثُقِفُوٓاْ إِلَّا بِحَبۡلٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَحَبۡلٖ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
They have been branded with humiliation wherever they are found, unless they
are protected by a covenant with Allāh or a covenant with people. (Sūrah Āl
ʿImrān, Q3:112)
\AYAH_END

This verse clarifies that there are only two ways by which the Jews may escape
humiliation. Either they embrace Islam, although \ITA{إِلَّا بِحَبْلٍ مِنَ
اللَّه} has also been interpreted in other ways by the exegetes, or they secure
the protection and patronage of others in worldly affairs.

The point is that they cannot attain national honour independently of external
support. Whether the establishment and continued existence of the State of
Israel rests upon its own intrinsic strength or upon the support of the Western
powers is a matter well known to all. Once this explanatory verse from Sūrah Āl
ʿImrān is taken into account, the meaning of the verse in Sūrah al-Baqarah is no
longer problematic.

Another example is the _āyah_ in Sūrah al-Māʾidah:

\AYAH_AR_START
إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَٱلَّذِينَ هَادُواْ وَٱلصَّٰبِـُٔونَ وَٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ مَنۡ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحٗا فَلَا خَوۡفٌ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا هُمۡ يَحۡزَنُونَ ٦٩
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Indeed, the believers, the Jews, the Sabians, and the Christians, whoever truly
believes in Allāh and the Last Day and does righteous deeds shall have no fear,
nor shall they grieve. (Sūrah al-Māʾidah, Q5:69)
\AYAH_END

In modern times, some have argued from this verse that belief in Allāh alone is
sufficient for salvation, and that belief in the Prophets, the angels, and the
revealed Scriptures is unnecessary, because the verse explicitly mentions only
belief in Allāh and the Last Day.

This misunderstanding arises precisely because related Quranic verses are
ignored when interpreting individual passages.

The reality is that the Quran unequivocally declares disbelief in the Messengers
to be disbelief itself. Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَكۡفُرُونَ بِٱللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِۦ وَيُرِيدُونَ أَن يُفَرِّقُواْ بَيۡنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِۦ وَيَقُولُونَ نُؤۡمِنُ بِبَعۡضٖ وَنَكۡفُرُ بِبَعۡضٖ وَيُرِيدُونَ أَن يَتَّخِذُواْ بَيۡنَ ذَٰلِكَ سَبِيلًا ١٥٠ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ حَقّٗاۚ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Indeed, those who disbelieve in Allāh and His Messengers, and seek to make a
distinction between Allāh and His Messengers, saying, "We believe in some and
reject others," wishing to adopt a way in between, it is they who are truly the
disbelievers. (Sūrah al-Nisāʾ, Q4:150--1)
\AYAH_END

In light of this explicit declaration, how can the verse in Sūrah al-Māʿidah be
understood to mean that belief in the Messengers is unnecessary?

The reality is that the verse in Sūrah al-Māʿidah is concise (_mujmal_). In
concise passages, the Quran often mentions only belief in Allāh and the Last
Day, whereas in detailed passages it sets forth the complete articles of faith.

Sūrah al-Taghābun provides examples of both styles. While explaining the
articles of faith, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
فَـَٔامِنُواْ بِٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦ وَٱلنُّورِ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنزَلۡنَاۚ وَٱللَّهُ بِمَا تَعۡمَلُونَ خَبِيرٞ ٨ يَوۡمَ يَجۡمَعُكُمۡ لِيَوۡمِ ٱلۡجَمۡعِۖ ذَٰلِكَ يَوۡمُ ٱلتَّغَابُنِۗ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
So believe in Allāh, His Messenger, and the Light We have sent down. Allāh is
All-Aware of what you do. The Day He gathers you for the Day of Gathering, that
will be the Day of Ultimate Gain and Loss. (Sūrah al-Taghābun, Q64:8--9)
\AYAH_END

Here, belief in Allāh, His Messenger \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}, the revealed Quran, and
the Hereafter are all explicitly mentioned. Thereafter, when referring to this
complete body of faith in summary form, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَمَن يُؤۡمِنۢ بِٱللَّهِ وَيَعۡمَلۡ صَٰلِحٗا
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Whoever believes in Allāh and does righteous deeds... (Sūrah al-Taghābun, Q64:9)
\AYAH_END

These are merely two examples intended to demonstrate how essential it is to
interpret the verses of the Noble Quran in the light of one another.

They also make it clear that a mere knowledge of the Arabic language is
insufficient for determining the intended meaning of the Quranic verses. Rather,
one must possess a comprehensive and penetrating understanding of the Quranic
sciences as a whole.

For this reason, not everyone who claims to understand the Quran can be accepted
as an authority.

The same advice should be offered to those who wish to benefit from the Noble
Quran through translations. They should read a translation accompanied by the
guidance of a qualified scholar, or together with reliable explanatory notes or
an authoritative Tafsīr. A translation by itself is not free from the risk of
misunderstanding, because it cannot ordinarily indicate matters such as _naskh_
(abrogation), passages that are _mujmal_ (concise) or _mufaṣṣal_ (detailed), texts
that are _muṭlaq_ (unrestricted) or _muqayyad_ (qualified), nor the historical
circumstances surrounding the revelation of particular verses.

Without knowledge of these matters, it is impossible to attain the intended
meaning and objective of the Noble Quran.
