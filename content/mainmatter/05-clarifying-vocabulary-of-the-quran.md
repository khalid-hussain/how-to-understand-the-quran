
# 5. Clarifying the Vocabulary of the Noble Quran

The soundest method for explaining the vocabulary of the Noble Quran is to begin
with the Quran itself, then the Sunnah, and thereafter the statements of the
Ṣaḥābah \hon{\P_THM}{\BODY} and the Tābiʿūn \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}.

Since the Quran was revealed in the Arabic language, there is, undoubtedly, no
alternative to consulting the Arabic language in explaining its vocabulary. Yet,
while recognising the indispensable importance of Arabic linguistics, one must
also bear in mind that the Quran possesses its own distinctive terminology.
These Quranic expressions can only be properly understood through the Quran and
the Sunnah themselves.

For example, the expression _al-Ṣirāṭ al-Mustaqīm_. Lexically, it means, "the
straight path." However, what does the Quran itself intend by this expression?

In Sūrat al-Fātiḥah, the Quran itself explains it:

\AYAH_AR_START
صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
The path of those upon whom You have bestowed Your favour. (Sūrah al-Fātiḥah,
Q1:7)
\AYAH_END

Who are those upon whom Allāh has bestowed His favour? This too is explained
elsewhere in the Quran:

<!-- TODO The translation "What excellent companions they are!" was not in the original Arabic but part of the ayah and the translation and so I added it. -->
\AYAH_AR_START
وَمَن يُطِعِ ٱللَّهَ وَٱلرَّسُولَ فَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهِم مِّنَ ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ وَٱلصِّدِّيقِينَ وَٱلشُّهَدَآءِ وَٱلصَّٰلِحِينَۚ وَحَسُنَ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ رَفِيقٗا ٦٩
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Whoever obeys Allāh and the Messenger will be among those whom Allāh has
blessed: the Prophets, the steadfast affirmers of truth, the martyrs, and the
righteous. What excellent companions they are! (Sūrah al-Nisāʾ, Q4:69)
\AYAH_END

It thus becomes clear that _al-Ṣirāṭ al-Mustaqīm_ refers to the path of the
Prophets \hon{\S_THM}{\BODY}, the Ṣiddīqūn, the martyrs, and the righteous.

Similarly, towards the end of Sūrah Āl ʿImrān, Allāh states that within the
creation of the heavens and the earth, and the alternation of night and day, are
signs for those of understanding. Here, the Quran uses the expression _Ulū
al-Albāb_.

Who are the _Ulū al-Albāb_ according to the Quran? The Quran itself explains
that they are those who remember Allāh while standing, sitting, and lying on
their sides, and who reflect upon the creation of the heavens and the earth.

Likewise, with regard to acts of worship, the Quran employs terms such as Ṣalāh,
Zakāh, Ṣawm, and Ḥajj.

Lexically:

- Ṣalāh means supplication.
- Zakāh means growth or purification.
- Ṣawm means abstention.
- Ḥajj means intention or purposeful journey.

Clearly, however, these lexical meanings are not what is intended in these
contexts. Each of these words carries a specific technical meaning, and it is
the Sunnah that defines those meanings.

If these technical meanings were disregarded, and one were to rely solely upon
the lexical meanings of the words, the entire structure of Islamic worship would
cease to exist.

<!-- TODO ref Tafsir al-Qayyim -->
It is for this reason that al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn al-Qayyim \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} said,
"The Noble Quran possesses its own established linguistic convention (_ʿurf_)
and its own defined meanings. It is not permissible to interpret the Quran
outside the framework of this convention." (Al-Tafsīr al-Qayyim)

Among the most distressing and painful phenomena witnessed in our own time is
that after studying only a few elementary Arabic readers, people begin to
imagine themselves qualified to understand the Quran, derive legal rulings from
it, and cite it as authoritative proof.

Such conduct reflects great audacity and profound irresponsibility.

<!-- TODO ref Ibn Taymiyyah Muqaddimah Usul al-Tafsir -->

Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymīyah \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, while discussing errors in
Tafsīr in his _Muqaddimah fī Uṣūl al-Tafsīr_, writes,

> Another group interpreted the Quran solely according to the Arabic language,
> without considering what the Speaker of the Quran intended, what the one upon
> whom it was revealed \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} explained, or how those who first
> received the Quran understood it. (Muqaddimah fī Uṣūl al-Tafsīr)

<!-- TODO ref Qurtubi Tafsir -->

Similarly, al-ʿAllāmah al-Qurṭubī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} writes,

> Whoever interprets the Quran solely on the basis of the Arabic language,
> without relying upon transmitted reports and established narrations, will
> inevitably fall into numerous errors, and will be guilty of interpreting the
> Quran according to personal opinion (_tafsīr bi al-raʾy_). (Al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām
> al-Quran)

Accordingly, anyone seeking to explain or interpret the vocabulary of the Noble
Quran requires not only a command of the Arabic language, but also a thorough
understanding of the Quran's own linguistic usage, its technical terminology,
and the explanations given by Muḥammad \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}.

Otherwise, the consequences can be exceedingly dangerous.

At this point, the following guidance of Shaykh Anwar Shāh Kashmīrī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} deserves the closest attention,

<!-- TODO ref Malfuzat Anwar Shah Kashmiri -->

> In its choice of vocabulary, the Noble Quran always selects the word that is
> both the most accurate in conveying the intended reality and the most
> appropriate to the context. For example, according to the beliefs of the
> pre-Islamic Arabs, it was not appropriate to apply the word _tawaffī_ to
> death, because they believed neither in the continued existence of the body
> nor in the survival of the soul. The word _tawaffī_ literally means, "to take
> in full," or, "to receive completely." According to their belief, death could
> not properly be described as _tawaffī_. The Noble Quran, however, deliberately
> employed this word to describe death, thereby demonstrating that death is a
> reception and transfer, not utter annihilation. Through a single word, the
> Quran unveiled this profound reality. Elsewhere, the same word is employed in
> its original sense of taking or receiving completely. (Malfūẓāt Anwar Shāh
> Kashmīrī)

The essence of Shaykh's observation is that every word employed by the Noble
Quran is chosen with extraordinary precision in relation to both its context and
intended meaning. At times, a single word encapsulates an entire theological or
legal principle. Consequently, the utmost care must be exercised in determining
and explaining the meanings of Quranic vocabulary.
