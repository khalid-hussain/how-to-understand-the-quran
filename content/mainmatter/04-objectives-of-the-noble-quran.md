
# The Objectives of the Noble Quran

The student of the Noble Quran must also have complete clarity regarding another
essential matter: In which matters do we seek guidance from the Quran? What is
the subject and central theme of the Quran?

The failure to resolve this question has deprived many travellers on this path
of reaching their intended destination. Mistaking a mirage for the water that
would quench their thirst, they ultimately gained nothing but bewilderment and
disappointment.

This reality must be understood well: the primary call of the Noble Quran is to
invite mankind to everlasting felicity. It seeks to cultivate both the outward
and inward dimensions of the human being so that he encounters no hardship in
the Hereafter. It seeks to purify the human soul so that it becomes worthy of
standing in the presence of Allāh.

Undoubtedly, the Noble Quran lays down the principles governing every aspect of
worldly life. It addresses the laws governing individual and collective life,
beliefs, acts of worship, moral conduct, social dealings, rights, and etiquette.
Yet, throughout all of these subjects, the fundamental perspective remains
success in the Hereafter.

It is for this reason that wherever these matters are discussed in the Quran,
one invariably finds, at the beginning, middle, or conclusion of the passage,
_āyāt_ of encouragement or warning, references to Paradise and Hellfire, or
mention of Divine punishment. Likewise, one or more of Allāh's Most Beautiful
Names are mentioned in accordance with the context, so that the reader
continually realizes that obedience to these commands leads to eternal bliss,
while disobedience results in everlasting disgrace in the Hereafter.

Imam al-Shāṭibī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} makes a profound observation in
_al-Muwāfaqāt_:

> The fundamental sciences of the Noble Quran are only three:

> 1. Knowledge of Allāh Most High;
> 2. The means by which His pleasure is attained; and
> 3. The ultimate destiny of mankind.

> The first category, knowledge of Allāh, includes knowledge of His Names,
> Attributes, and Actions. Discussion of Prophethood also falls under this
> heading, since the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} is the intermediary between the
> servant and his Lord.

> The second category includes acts of worship, social transactions, and similar
> matters.

> The third category includes death and its circumstances, the Day of Resurrection
> and all that it encompasses, the conditions of Paradise and Hellfire, as well as
> the _āyāt_ of glad tidings and warning, and those passages describing the
> salvation of the righteous and the evil end of the wicked.

The point is that the principal subject of the Noble Quran is to guide mankind
towards living in this world in such a manner that life in the Hereafter becomes
successful and the pleasure of Allāh becomes their ultimate attainment.

The Noble Quran has introduced itself to mankind with precisely this message,
repeatedly drawing attention to this very objective. Consider the following
_āyāt_:

In summary, the commands, teachings, exhortations, and guidance of the Noble
Quran constitute its distinctive characteristics and the very essence of its
message. It is for this reason that a number of leading scholars, among them
Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymīyah \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} and Imam Shāh Walī Allāh
al-Dihlawī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, regarded this as the primary aspect of the
Quran's miraculous nature (_iʿjāz_). Accordingly, anyone seeking to benefit from
the Noble Quran should reflect upon its _āyāt_ from this perspective.

Imam Shāh Walī Allāh \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} states explicitly in _al-Fawz
al-Kabīr_, "The established reality is that the primary purpose behind the
revelation of the Quran is the moral and spiritual refinement of mankind, and
the eradication of their false beliefs and corrupt practices."[-@@fawz_kabir, p. 19]

He expresses the same idea in _al-Tafhīmāt_ in the following words, "Allāh Most
High granted me, in a few concise words, an understanding whose essence is this:
true faith is embedded within every human heart. However, the intoxication of
material life has overwhelmed mankind. Therefore, Allāh revealed the Noble Quran
so that, through it, these worldly inclinations might be subdued."[-@@tafheemat,
vol. 2, p. 122]

The meaning is that the primary objective of the Noble Quran is to establish the
relationship between the servant and his Lord upon sound foundations, and to
make worldly life the preparation for the life of the Hereafter.

How unfortunate it is, therefore, that people search the Noble Quran for
discussions of ancient and modern philosophy, astronomical theories, scientific
discoveries, and the details of history and geography, and then seek to judge
the truthfulness and greatness of the Quran according to these standards.

We do not deny that such enquiries may yield incidental benefits. Nevertheless,
it is evident that these subjects do not constitute the purpose of the Quran,
whose rank is far higher and more exalted than this.

the manner in which it does so is fundamentally different. Imam Shāh Walī Allāh
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} explains,

even expresses concern regarding those who become excessively preoccupied with
purely religious disciplines to such an extent that the true objective is lost.
While discussing the narratives of the Quran, he writes,

> …nor did He (Allāh) recount the stories in their entirety, with all their
> particular details.

> The wisdom behind this is that when ordinary people hear an exceptionally
> unusual story, or when a story is related to them with every detail and

> itself, causing them to lose sight of the primary purpose, which is to serve
> as a reminder (_tadhkīr_).

> An example of this is the saying attributed to one of the people of spiritual
> insight: “When people began memorizing the rules of Quranic recitation, they
> became so preoccupied with them that they lost the reverence and humility they

> virtually nonexistent.”[-@@fawz_kabir, pp. 45--6]

The student of the Noble Quran must therefore exercise great caution in this
regard. His attention should remain fixed upon the true subject and objective of
the Quran, so that he may attain the precious jewel for which he seeks.
