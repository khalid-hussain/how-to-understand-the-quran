
# 9. Tafsīr bi al-Raʾy (Interpretation Based on Personal Opinion)

From the foregoing discussion, it has become clear that the primary method of
interpreting the Noble Quran is to explain one Quranic _āyah_ by means of
another. Thereafter, authentic _aḥādīth_ should be consulted.

Furthermore, if the meanings of the Noble Quran are explained in accordance with
the principles of the Arabic language and in conformity with the principles of
the Sharīʿah, this too constitutes a sound and legitimate method of Tafsīr.

However, if the meanings of the Quranic verses are explained without regard for
the principles of the Sharīʿah or the rules of the Arabic language, such
interpretation is impermissible. This is what is known as _Tafsīr bi al-Raʾy_.

<!-- TODO ref Jami3 al-Tirmidhī -->
It is narrated in _Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī_ that the Messenger of Allāh
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} said, "Whoever interprets the Quran according to his own
opinion, let him prepare his seat in the Fire." (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī and Musnad
Aḥmad)

<!-- TODO ref Sharh al-Sunnah -->
Another narration states, "Whoever says anything concerning the Quran without
knowledge, let him prepare his seat in the Fire." (Sharḥ al-Sunnah) The wording
is also reported in the collections of Imams Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, and others
through various routes.

<!-- TODO check how to refer to a word -->
Here, _knowledge_ refers to knowledge of the principles of the Arabic language
and the principles of the Sharīʿah.

Undoubtedly, it is ḥarām (unlawful) for anyone who is ignorant of these two
sciences to undertake the interpretation of the Noble Quran.

<!-- TODO ref muwafaqat Shatibi -->
Al-Shāṭibī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} states in _al-Muwāfaqāt_, "Opinion (_raʾy_) is of
two kinds. The first is an opinion that accords with the Book, the Sunnah, and
the principles of the Arabic language. Such opinion cannot be disregarded or
neglected. The second is an opinion that neither conforms to the revealed proofs
nor accords with the rules of the Arabic language. Without doubt, this is the
opinion that is blameworthy."

Similarly, al-Jaṣṣāṣ al-Rāzī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} writes in _Aḥkām al-Qurʾān_
regarding the Prophetic statement, "Whoever interprets the Quran according to
his own opinion, even if he happens to be correct, has nevertheless erred."
(Sunan Abī Dāwūd), He explains,

> This refers to the person who departs from the established principles of
> interpretation and merely says whatever occurs to his mind concerning the
> meaning of the Quran.

> As for the one who explains the meanings of the Quranic verses and understands
> them according to meanings upon which the scholars are agreed, such a person is
> praiseworthy, deserving of reward, and included among those concerning whom
> Allāh says,
