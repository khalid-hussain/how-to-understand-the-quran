
# A Sound Grasp of the Arabic Language

Since the Noble Quran was revealed in Arabic, there is no alternative for
anyone seeking to benefit from it but to acquire familiarity with the Arabic
language. At the same time, it should never be forgotten that the linguistic
standard of the Quran is so lofty that many leading Muslim scholars regarded
its eloquence (_faṣāḥah_) and rhetorical excellence (_balāghah_) to be the primary
aspect of its miraculous nature (_iʿjāz_).

Accordingly, one should never rely upon a superficial knowledge of Quranic
vocabulary, sentence structure, or style. Rather, one should consult the
foremost authorities on Arabic lexicography and the rhetorical styles of the
language so that the meanings of the Noble Quran may be understood correctly.

In this regard, the following observations deserve particular attention.

## Lexical Works on the Vocabulary of the Quran

In explaining the vocabulary of the Noble Quran, scholars have adopted two
principal approaches.

Some sought to cover every Quranic word. Their works are generally known as
_Mufradāt al-Qurʾān_, the most famous example being _Mufradāt Alfāẓ al-Qurʾān_ by
Imam al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}.

Others confined themselves to explaining only the difficult expressions of the
Quran, calling this discipline _Gharīb al-Qurʾān_.

Many distinguished scholars devoted themselves to this field. Among those who
made the greatest contribution were Ibn Durayd \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} and his
student al-ʿAzīzī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}. Teacher and student together are said to
have spent fifteen years compiling their work on _Gharīb al-Qurʾān_.

## _Wujūh_ and _Naẓāʾir_

Many words in the Quran occur in multiple places while carrying different
meanings. The scholars of rhetoric refer to such words as _mushtarak_, whereas the
scholars of ʿUlūm al-Quran call them _naẓāʾir_.

Conversely, there are words that appear in numerous places yet consistently
retain the same meaning. These are known among the scholars of the Quran as
_wujūh_.

Familiarity with _wujūh_ and _naẓāʾir_ is indispensable for understanding the
meanings of the Quran, lest one confuse the intended sense of a word.

For this reason, Muslim scholars authored independent works devoted to this
subject. Among them is al-Ḥāfiẓ Jalāl al-Dīn al-Suyūṭī's \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}
treatise _Maʿtarak al-Aqrān fī Mushtarak al-Qurʾān_.

## The Importance of _Iʿrāb_

Arabic expresses the grammatical relationships between words through _iʿrāb_,
namely the inflectional vowel endings.

Through _iʿrāb_, one distinguishes the subject (_fāʿil_), object (_mafʿūl bihi_),
possessive construction (_muḍāf_ and _muḍāf ilayh_), circumstantial qualifier
(_ḥāl_), specification (_tamyīz_), and many other grammatical functions.

It is therefore evident how essential knowledge of _iʿrāb_ is for understanding
meaning.

The scholars of Islam recognized this need and produced numerous works devoted
exclusively to the grammatical analysis of the Noble Quran, examining each
_sūrah_ _āyah_ by _āyah_.

## The Rhetorical Structure of Quranic Expression

The student must also understand the methods by which the Noble Quran conveys
its meanings, together with the functions of the various particles,
prepositions, and connecting expressions that it employs.

Likewise, one must appreciate the significance of word order (_taqdīm_ and
_taʾkhīr_), definiteness and indefiniteness (_taʿrīf_ and _tankīr_),
unrestricted and qualified expressions (_iṭlāq_ and _taqyīd_), and similar
rhetorical devices.

Without an understanding of these matters, the meanings of the Quran cannot be
properly grasped.

Accordingly, the masters of Arabic literature, who were best qualified to
address these subjects, authored numerous works under the general heading of
_Maʿānī al-Qurʾān_. Familiarity with this discipline is essential for every serious
student of the Quran.

A single example illustrates how subtle these matters can be. Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَإِذۡ فَرَقۡنَا بِكُمُ ٱلۡبَحۡرَ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
And remember when We parted the sea for you. (Sūrat al-Baqarah, Q2:50)
\AYAH_END

At first glance, one might object that the _āyah_ addresses the Jews living
during the Prophet's \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} lifetime, although the event occurred to
their forefathers.

Various explanations have been offered. Al-Ālūsī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, however,
points out in _Rūḥ al-Maʿānī_ that had the _āyah_ said \ITA{لَكُم}, it would have
referred directly to the contemporaneous Jews. Instead, the use of the
preposition \ITA{ب} in \ITA{بِكُم} carries the sense that the sea was parted on
behalf of their forefathers. Thus, the subtle choice of a single particle
removes the apparent difficulty.[-@@ruhMa3ani, vol. 1, p. 256]

## Literal and Figurative Expression

When a word is used in the meaning for which it was originally coined, it is
called _ḥaqīqah_ (literal usage). When it is employed to convey a related but
non-literal meaning, it is called _majāz_ (figurative usage).

The Noble Quran contains numerous instances of figurative expression, and
familiarity with them is indispensable for the exegete.

Muslim scholars therefore authored independent works entitled _Majāz al-Qurʾān_.

Among the finest is _al-Ishārah ilá al-Ījāz fī Baʿḍ Anwāʿ al-Majāz_ by Sulṭān
al-ʿUlamāʾ al-ʿIzz ibn ʿAbd al-Salām \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} (d.660\SC{h}), in which
the Quranic _āyāt_ are examined comprehensively and their figurative meanings
carefully explained.

## Simile in the Quran

Many meanings are too subtle to be explained directly. Their clarification would
require lengthy discussion. The most concise and effective method is therefore
to express them through _tashbīh_ (simile), by likening hidden realities to
familiar and observable ones, enabling the listener to arrive at the intended
meaning through comparison.

The Noble Quran employs numerous such similes.

Comprehensive discussions of these occur in the classical works on rhetoric,
_Maʿānī al-Quran_, the miraculous nature of the Quran, and _Majāz al-Qurʾān_.
Their study is indispensable for every student of the Quran.

## The Significance of Morphological Patterns

Throughout the Noble Quran, words derived from the same root appear in
different _abwāb_ (verbal forms).

Clearly, these variations are never devoid of semantic significance.

The general principle is that an increase in the form of a word usually conveys
an increase in meaning and emphasis.

As the author of _al-Ṭirāz_ writes, "The strength of an expression in conveying
a meaning comes from shifting it from one form to another with more letters. The
meaning is thereby intensified through the added letters; otherwise, the
addition of letters would be pointless and serve no purpose."[-@@tiraz, vol. 2,
p. 86]

The following examples illustrate this principle.

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The phrases \ITA{عَلَّامُ الغُيُوب} (_ʿAllām al-Ghuyūb_) and \ITA{عَالِمُ الغُيُوب} (_ʿĀlim al-Ghuyūb_)
:::

There is a considerable difference between these two expressions.

_ʿAllām_ denotes One who possesses complete and all-encompassing knowledge,
whereas _ʿĀlim_ simply signifies One who knows.

Likewise, there is an evident distinction between _Qādir_ and _Muqtadir_.

Similarly, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلتَّوَّٰبِينَ وَيُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُتَطَهِّرِينَ ٢٢٢
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Indeed, Allāh loves those who constantly repent and those who continually purify
themselves. (Sūrat al-Baqarah, Q2:222)
\AYAH_END

The emphasis conveyed by _al-tawwābīn_ and _al-mutaṭahhirīn_ would not have been
achieved had the _āyah_ simply said _al-tāʾibīn_ and _al-ṭāhirīn_.

_Tawwāb_ denotes one who repeatedly returns in repentance, whereas _tāʾib_
merely signifies one who repents.[-@@tiraz, vol. 2, p. 87]

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The words \ITA{اسْطَاعُواْ} (_isṭāʿū_) and \ITA{اسْتَطَاعُواْ} (_istaṭāʿū_)
:::

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
فَمَا ٱسۡطَٰعُوٓاْ أَن يَظۡهَرُوهُ وَمَا ٱسۡتَطَٰعُواْ لَهُۥ نَقۡبٗا ٩٧
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
So they could neither scale it nor pierce it. (Sūrat al-Kahf, Q18:97)
\AYAH_END

The variation between \ITA{اسْطَاعُواْ} (_isṭāʿū_) and \ITA{اسْتَطَاعُواْ}
(_istaṭāʿū_) is meaningful.

The additional letter \ITA{ت} conveys greater effort and intensity.
Consequently, the lighter form \ITA{اسْطَاعُواْ} is used for the comparatively
easier act of climbing the barrier, whereas the heavier form \ITA{اسْتَطَاعُواْ}
is employed for the far more difficult task of piercing through
it.[-@@taj_mukallal, p. 191]

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The word \ITA{تَخْتَانُونَ} (_takhtānūn_)
:::

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
عَلِمَ ٱللَّهُ أَنَّكُمۡ كُنتُمۡ تَخۡتَانُونَ أَنفُسَكُمۡ فَتَابَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ وَعَفَا عَنكُمۡۖ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Allāh knew that you had been betraying yourselves, so He turned to you in mercy
and pardoned you. (Sūrat al-Baqarah, Q2:187)
\AYAH_END

The occurrence of \ITA{تَخْتَانُونَ} (_takhtānūn_) in Form VIII (_iftiʿāl_) is highly significant.

Some deviant groups exploited this _āyah_ to cast aspersions upon the Ṣaḥābah
\hon{\P_THM}{\BODY}.

However, the Arabic lexicons show that _ikhtiyān_ denotes the inclination or
intention to betray, whereas the ordinary act of betrayal is expressed by
_khiyānah_.

The mere occurrence of an inclination or passing thought is not blameworthy, for
Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
إِنَّ ٱلنَّفۡسَ لَأَمَّارَةُۢ بِٱلسُّوٓءِ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Surely the human soul persistently inclines towards evil. (Sūrat Yūsuf, Q12:53)
\AYAH_END

Thus, intending an action is one thing, while actually committing it is another.

The _āyah_ refers only to the former, not the latter. Consequently, it
constitutes one of the virtues of the Ṣaḥābah \hon{\P_THM}{\BODY}, for despite
the natural inclination, they refrained from committing the prohibited
act.[-@@mufradat_raghib, vol. 1, p. 217]

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The words \ITA{كَسَبَتْ} (_kasabat_) and \ITA{اكْتَسَبَتْ} (_iktasabat_)
:::

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتۡ وَعَلَيۡهَا مَا ٱكۡتَسَبَتۡۗ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
It will be rewarded for whatever good it earns, and held accountable for
whatever evil it deliberately commits. (Sūrat al-Baqarah, Q2:286)
\AYAH_END

A profound distinction is conveyed here by the addition of only a single letter.
The verb \ITA{اكْتَسَبَتْ} (_iktasabat_), being from Form VIII (_iftiʿāl_),
carries the implication of deliberate intention and conscious effort.
Accordingly, good deeds earn reward whenever they are performed, whereas
punishment for sin is attached only to that which is committed
intentionally.[-@@tiraz, vol. 2, p. 87]

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The word \ITA{حَصُورٌ} (_ḥaṣūr_)
:::

Regarding Prophet Yaḥyá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY}, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَسَيِّدٗا وَحَصُورٗا وَنَبِيّٗا مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ ٣٩
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
...[will be] a great leader, chaste, and a prophet among the righteous. (Sūrat
Āl ʿImrān, Q3:39)
\AYAH_END

The root _ḥaṣr_ means restraint or prevention.

However, \ITA{حَصُورٌ} (_ḥaṣūr_) is an intensive form.

Al-Khafājī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} explains, "Since _ḥaṣūr_ is an intensive form,
and intensity applies only to voluntary actions, it signifies one who
voluntarily exercises restraint despite possessing the ability."[^khafaji]

Thus, Prophet Yaḥyá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} was not compelled to remain celibate.
Rather, it was a conscious act of voluntary self-restraint.

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The word \ITA{تَنَزَّلُ} (_tanazzalu_)
:::

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
تَنَزَّلُ ٱلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
The angels and the Spirit descend therein. (Sūrat al-Qadr, Q97:4)
\AYAH_END

The verb \ITA{تَنَزَّلُ} _tanazzalu_ belongs to Form V (_tafaʿʿul_), whose
characteristic is gradual occurrence.

The author of _al-Futūḥāt al-Ilāhīyah_ observes that this indicates the angels
do not descend all at once. Rather, they continue descending in successive
groups throughout the night, just as pilgrims arrive at the Kaʿbah one group
after another.[-@@futuhatilahiyah, vol. 8, p. 370]

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The word \ITA{المُعَذِّرُونَ} (_al-muʿadhdhirūn_)
:::

Allāh says concerning the hypocrites,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَجَآءَ ٱلۡمُعَذِّرُونَ مِنَ ٱلۡأَعۡرَابِ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Some nomadic Arabs (also) came with excuses. (Sūrat al-Tawbah, Q9:90)
\AYAH_END

The Quran deliberately says \ITA{المُعَذِّرُونَ} (_al-muʿadhdhirūn_), not
\ITA{المُعْتَذِرُونَ} (_al-muʿtadhirūn_). The difference is significant.

_Muʿadhdhir_ refers to one who presents an excuse despite not truly possessing
one, whereas _muʿtadhir_ may refer to one who has either a genuine or an invalid
excuse. By choosing \ITA{المُعَذِّرُونَ}, the Quran indicates that the hypocrites were
not genuinely excused.[-@@futuhatilahiyah, vol. 3, p. 295]

::: {custom-style="Example Heading"}
The word \ITA{يُطِيقُونَهُ}
:::

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَعَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُۥ فِدۡيَةٞ طَعَامُ مِسۡكِينٖۖ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
For those who can only fast with extreme difficulty, compensation can be made by
feeding a needy person (for every day not fasted). (Sūrat al-Baqarah, Q2:184)
\AYAH_END

The exegetical discussions surrounding this _āyah_ are well known.

However, if one reflects upon the occurrence of \ITA{يُطِيقُونَهُ} in Form IV
(_ifʿāl_), the matter becomes considerably easier.

One recognized function of this form is privation (_salb_), from which some
scholars understood \ITA{يُطِيقُونَهُ} (_yuṭīqūnah_) in the sense of \ITA{لَا
يُطِيقُونَهُ} (_lā yuṭīqūnah_).

Accordingly, the _āyah_ would mean: Those who are unable to fast should instead
offer a ransom by feeding a poor person.

The author of _Lisān al-ʿArab_ explains that _ṭawq_ and _iṭāqah_ denote the
ability to endure something only with hardship and difficulty.

On this basis, the _āyah_ signifies that those who are physically capable of
fasting, but only with great hardship and difficulty, are permitted to refrain
from fasting and instead offer the _fidyah_ (prescribed compensation).

This understanding is also supported in the commentary on _Sunan Abī Dāwūd_, where
_iṭāqah_ is explained as referring to those who possess the ability to fast only
with considerable hardship. Such individuals are therefore permitted to forgo
the fast and pay the _fidyah_ instead.
