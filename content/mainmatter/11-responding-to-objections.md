
# Responding to Objections

Followers of other religions have also studied the Noble Quran. However, their
objective has generally been to raise objections, or to exploit Quranic verses
and expressions in support of their own beliefs and thereby mislead Muslims.

For this reason, the student of the Noble Quran must exercise great caution in
this regard.

To illustrate this point, we shall present only a single example.

The Noble Quran refers to Prophet ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as the Word of
Allāh. Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَكَلِمَتُهُۥٓ أَلۡقَىٰهَآ إِلَىٰ مَرۡيَمَ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
...and His Word, which He conveyed to Mary... (Sūrah al-Nisāʾ, Q4:171)
\AYAH_END

Christians have repeatedly attempted to use this expression to cast Muslims into
confusion.

Even during the Miḥnah (the controversy over the createdness of the Quran) in
the Abbasid period, this very expression became the centre of debate.

The creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jamāʿah is that the Speech of Allāh (Kalām
Allāh) is uncreated.

The Christians objected by arguing that if the Speech of Allāh is uncreated, and
ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} is described as the Word of Allāh, then ʿĪsá
\hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} must also be uncreated.

It appears that one of the reasons the Muʿtazilah insisted so strongly that the
Speech of Allāh was created was to escape precisely this Christian objection.

Ironically, the Christians used the title _Kalimat Allāh_ to argue that ʿĪsá
\hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} was uncreated, whereas the Jaḥmīyah employed the very same
expression to argue that the Quran was created.

Their reasoning was as follows: The Quran calls ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY}
_Kalimat Allāh_ (The Word of Allāh), yet ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} is a created
being. Therefore, the Speech of Allāh must also be created.

Imam Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} refuted both the Christian and the
Jahmī arguments. He observed that numerous qualities attributed to ʿĪsá
\hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} cannot possibly be attributed to the Quran.

ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} was an infant. He grew into adulthood. He ate and
drank. He was subject to the commands and prohibitions of Allāh. He was among
the descendants of Prophet Nūḥ \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} and Prophet Ibrāhīm
\hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY}.

Can any of these attributes be ascribed to the Noble Quran? The conclusion,
therefore, is that it is fundamentally unsound to draw an analogy between the
Noble Quran and ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY}.

As for the Quranic description of ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as _Kalimat Allāh_,
its meaning is that he came into existence through Allāh's creative command,
"Kun" ("Be"), not that he is literally the Divine Word itself.[-@@jawabSahih,
vol. 2, pp. 490--3]

The reality of the matter is that after the Christians had lost the purity of
their original religion, they sought to attract followers of other religions and
philosophical schools by introducing sufficient flexibility into Christian
doctrine to accommodate people of diverse beliefs.

Thus, when they observed that certain philosophers upheld the doctrine of the
unity of the intellect, the intelligent, and the intelligible, they developed
the theological formulation of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

Classical Greek philosophy likewise affirmed the existence of a primordial
entity known as the Logos, through whom God supposedly brought the universe into
existence. The philosophers referred to this as the First Intellect.

The Christians appropriated this philosophical concept of the Logos and
superimposed it upon ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY}, thereby attempting to
incorporate elements of pagan Greek philosophy into Christianity. In doing so,
however, they themselves fell into error.

<!-- TODO Check if comma needed after "expression" -->
Later, when they sought to influence Muslims, they attempted to express this
same philosophical notion of the Logos through the Quranic expression Kalimat
Allāh.

It is evident, however, that when the Noble Quran describes ʿĪsá
\hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as the Word of Allāh, it bears no connection whatsoever to
the later Christian doctrine of the Logos.

Indeed, even the original religion of ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} is entirely free
from such beliefs.

All the theological doctrines that Christians subsequently developed under the
title of _Kalimat Allāh_ were borrowed from the pagan philosophical traditions
of Egypt and Greece, and are fundamentally incompatible with pure Tawḥīd.

## The Meaning of _Kalimat Allāh_ in the Quran

It now remains to examine how the Noble Quran itself employs the word _kalimah_.
In the Quran, the term _kalimah_ is applied to a complete statement or
declaration.

At times, it simply carries the meaning of a statement or utterance. Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَيُنذِرَ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُواْ ٱتَّخَذَ ٱللَّهُ وَلَدٗا ٤ مَّا لَهُم بِهِۦ مِنۡ عِلۡمٖ وَلَا لِأٓبَآئِهِمۡۚ كَبُرَتۡ كَلِمَةٗ تَخۡرُجُ مِنۡ أَفۡوَٰهِهِمۡۚ إِن يَقُولُونَ إِلَّا كَذِبٗا ٥
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
...and to warn those who say, "Allāh has taken a son." They have no knowledge of
this, nor did their forefathers. Grave is the word that comes out of their
mouths. They speak nothing but a lie. (Sūrah al-Kahf, Q18:4--5)
\AYAH_END

In this _āyah_, the Quran describes the statement:

\AYAH_AR_START
ٱتَّخَذَ ٱللَّهُ وَلَدٗا
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
"Allāh has taken a son,"
\AYAH_END

...as a _kalimah_. Likewise, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
قُلۡ يَٰٓأَهۡلَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ تَعَالَوۡاْ إِلَىٰ كَلِمَةٖ سَوَآءِۭ بَيۡنَنَا وَبَيۡنَكُمۡ أَلَّا نَعۡبُدَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ وَلَا نُشۡرِكَ بِهِۦ شَيۡـٔٗا وَلَا يَتَّخِذَ بَعۡضُنَا بَعۡضًا أَرۡبَابٗا مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِۚ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Say, "O People of the Book! Come to a common word between us and you: that we
worship none but Allāh, associate nothing with Him, and that none of us takes
others as lords besides Allāh." (Sūrah Āl ʿImrān, Q3:64)
\AYAH_END

Here, the entire statement beginning with \ITA{أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ}
is described as a _kalimah_.

Similarly, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا جَآءَ أَحَدَهُمُ ٱلۡمَوۡتُ قَالَ رَبِّ ٱرۡجِعُونِ ٩٩ لَعَلِّيٓ أَعۡمَلُ صَٰلِحٗا فِيمَا تَرَكۡتُۚ كـَلَّآۚ إِنَّهَا كَلِمَةٌ هُوَ قَآئِلُهَاۖ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Until, when death comes to one of them, he says, "My Lord! Send me back, so that
I may do righteous deeds in what I have left behind." No! It is only a word that
he utters... (Sūrah al-Muʾminūn, Q23:99--100)
\AYAH_END

Here again, the complete statement \ITA{رَبِّ ٱرۡجِعُونِ ٩٩ لَعَلِّيٓ أَعۡمَلُ
صَٰلِحٗا} is referred to as a _kalimah_.

A second meaning of _kalimah_ in the Noble Quran is that of a settled decree or
a divinely determined matter, namely something that has already been decided in
the eternal knowledge of Allāh.

The following verses clearly demonstrate this usage. Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَلَقَدۡ سَبَقَتۡ كَلِمَتُنَا لِعِبَادِنَا ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ ١٧١ إِنَّهُمۡ لَهُمُ ٱلۡمَنصُورُونَ ١٧٢ وَإِنَّ جُندَنَا لَهُمُ ٱلۡغَٰلِبُونَ ١٧٣
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Our decree had already gone forth in favour of Our Messengers, that they would
surely be granted victory, and that Our forces would indeed prevail. (Sūrah
al-Ṣāffāt, Q37:171--173)
\AYAH_END

<!-- TODO Check punctuation rules -->

The verse shows that the victory of the Messengers and the triumph of Allāh\'s
hosts had already been decreed. This predetermined decree is expressed through
the words \ITA{وَلَقَدۡ سَبَقَتۡ كَلِمَتُنَا}. Likewise, Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَكَذَٰلِكَ حَقَّتۡ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓاْ أَنَّهُمۡ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلنَّارِ ٦
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Thus the decree of your Lord became binding upon those who disbelieved, that
they are the people of the Fire. (Sūrah Ghāfir, Q40:6)
\AYAH_END

In other words, the destiny of the disbelievers as inhabitants of the Fire is a
decree already established with Allāh. This is expressed by the phrase \ITA{حَقَّتْ
كَلِمَةُ رَبِّك}. Allāh also says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَأَوۡرَثۡنَا ٱلۡقَوۡمَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَانُواْ يُسۡتَضۡعَفُونَ مَشَٰرِقَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَمَغَٰرِبَهَا ٱلَّتِي بَٰرَكۡنَا فِيهَاۖ وَتَمَّتۡ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ ٱلۡحُسۡنَىٰ عَلَىٰ بَنِيٓ إِسۡرَٰٓءِيلَ بِمَا صَبَرُواْۖ
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
We made the oppressed people inherit the eastern and western regions of the land
which We had blessed. Thus the gracious promise of your Lord was fulfilled for
the Children of Israel because they remained steadfast. (Sūrah al-Aʿrāf, Q7:137)
\AYAH_END

Thus, the triumph of the Children of Israel over the Egyptians, and their
inheritance of the land as a result of their perseverance, was something already
decreed by Allāh. This predetermined decree is expressed by \ITA{وَتَمَّتْ
كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ الْحُسْنَى}.

Similarly, regarding those who persist in rebellion and transgression, Allāh
says,

\AYAH_AR_START
كَذَٰلِكَ حَقَّتۡ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ فَسَقُوٓاْ أَنَّهُمۡ لَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ ٣٣
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
Thus the decree of your Lord became binding upon those who defiantly disobeyed,
that they would not believe. (Sūrah Yūnus, Q10:33)
\AYAH_END

In other words, it had already been decreed by Allāh that such rebellious people
would not come to faith.

In light of these explanations, the Quranic description of ʿĪsá
\hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as Kalimat Allāh becomes perfectly clear. Its meaning is
that his birth without a father was a matter already decreed in the eternal
knowledge of Allāh.

The Noble Quran makes this meaning entirely explicit by describing it as an _amr
maqḍī_ (a settled decree).

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
قَالَتۡ أَنَّىٰ يَكُونُ لِي غُلَٰمٞ وَلَمۡ يَمۡسَسۡنِي بَشَرٞ وَلَمۡ أَكُ بَغِيّٗا ٢٠ قَالَ كَذَٰلِكِ قَالَ رَبُّكِ هُوَ عَلَيَّ هَيِّنٞۖ وَلِنَجۡعَلَهُۥٓ ءَايَةٗ لِّلنَّاسِ وَرَحۡمَةٗ مِّنَّاۚ وَكَانَ أَمۡرٗا مَّقۡضِيّٗا ٢١
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
She said, "How can I have a son when no man has ever touched me, nor have I
been unchaste?" He replied, "So it will be. Your Lord says, 'It is easy for
Me. We shall make him a sign for mankind and a mercy from Us. And this has
already been decreed.'" (Sūrah Maryam, Q19:20--21)
\AYAH_END

This clearly demonstrates that, according to the terminology of the Noble Quran,
ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} is called Kalimat Allāh because his miraculous birth in
this manner was a divinely decreed and predetermined matter.


## Kalimat Allāh in the Bible

The Arabic translations of the Bible also contain passages in which the word
_kalimah_ is used in precisely the same sense as it is in the Noble Quran, namely,
to mean a statement, a command, or a divine decree.

A few examples are given below.

\BIBLE_AR_START
بِكَلِمَةِ الرَّبِّ صُنِعَتِ السَّمَاوَاتُ
\BIBLE_AR_END

\BIBLE_START
By the word of the Lord the heavens were made. (Psalms, 33:6)
\BIBLE_END


\BIBLE_AR_START
حَلَّتْ كَلِمَةُ اللَّهِ عَلَى نَاثَانَ النَّبِيِّ
\BIBLE_AR_END

\BIBLE_START
The word of God came to Nathan the Prophet. (1 Chronicles 17:3)
\BIBLE_END


\BIBLE_AR_START
كَلِمَةُ الرَّبِّ الَّتِي صَارَتْ إِلَى هُوشَعَ
\BIBLE_AR_END

\BIBLE_START
The word of the Lord that came to Hosea. (The Book of Hosea, Chapter 1)
\BIBLE_END


\BIBLE_AR_START
حَلَّتْ كَلِمَةُ اللَّهِ عَلَى يُوحَنَّا بْنِ زَكَرِيَّا
\BIBLE_AR_END

\BIBLE_START
The word of God came to John, the son of Zechariah. (The Gospel according to
Luke, Chapter 3, Verse 2)
\BIBLE_END


These examples demonstrate that the expression kalimah in the Arabic Bible is
likewise employed in the sense of a divine communication, command, or decree,
just as it is used in the Noble Quran.

## Kalimat Allāh and the Creative Command

It should also be borne in mind that the exegetes generally explain the birth of
ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as having occurred through the Divine creative command
(_kalimat al-takwīn_), namely, "Kun" ("Be"), just as the entire creation comes
into existence through this command.

Allāh says,

\AYAH_AR_START
وَإِذَا قَضَىٰٓ أَمۡرٗا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ ١١٧
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
...and whenever He decrees a matter, He only says to it, "Be," and it comes to
be. (Sūrah al-Baqarah, Q2:117)
\AYAH_END

For this reason, Prophet ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} is described as Kalimat Allāh.

This demonstrates that Kalimat Allāh refers to that very divine decree, while
the _kalimat al-takwīn_ ("Be") is the expression by which Allāh brings that
decree into effect.

Shāh Walī Allāh al-Dihlawī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} likewise explains the _kalimat
al-takwīn_ as Allāh's voluntary exercise of His will and power in bringing things
into existence.[-@@fawz_kabir, p. 22]

The essence of this discussion is that the Noble Quran does not describe Prophet
ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as Kalimat Allāh because he is the Divine Attribute of
Speech (Kalām Allāh) subsisting with the Divine Essence, nor because he forms
part of the Divine Essence, as Christians claim.

Rather, he is called Kalimat Allāh because his extraordinary birth from Maryam
\hon{\S_HER}{\BODY} without a father was a divinely decreed matter, brought into
existence solely by the command of Allāh.

A further question may be asked: Why did Allāh, in particular, describe the
birth of Prophet ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} as His predetermined decree and
attribute the spirit of life bestowed upon him to Himself?

The answer is that the Jews levelled grievous accusations against both Maryam
\hon{\S_HER}{\BODY} and Prophet ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY}, alleging, Allāh
forbid, that Maryam \hon{\S_HER}{\BODY} had committed an act of immorality and
that Prophet ʿĪsá \hon{\S_HIM}{\BODY} was born illegitimately.

In order to refute these accusations and publicly affirm the purity and chastity
of both mother and son, Allāh declared that his birth occurred solely through
His own creative decree and that the spirit of life bestowed upon him came from
Him.

Regarding Maryam \hon{\S_HER}{\BODY}, Allāh says:

\AYAH_AR_START
وَمَرۡيَمَ ٱبۡنَتَ عِمۡرَٰنَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَحۡصَنَتۡ فَرۡجَهَا فَنَفَخۡنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتۡ بِكَلِمَٰتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَكَانَتۡ مِنَ ٱلۡقَٰنِتِينَ ١٢
\AYAH_AR_END

\AYAH_START
And Mary, daughter of ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity, so We breathed into her
through Our Spirit. She affirmed the words of her Lord and His Scriptures, and
she was among the devoutly obedient. (Sūrah al-Taḥrīm, Q66:12)
\AYAH_END
